# Track B: performance log

**Result:** the reference takes **56.35 s / 2,200 MB** on the board. This design (`s2-y`) has a median K of **44.35** over 5 board runs (`run-09eb7dfb83e3`, `run-308cd4b414d9`, `run-15c9c75ad33c`, `run-0d4ba97358a4`, `run-06a4871e5ef2`), at S 1,077, with T 0.0653 s and M 31.8 MB at the median. Its best run, `run-06a4871e5ef2`, read **0.0645 s / 33.7 MB**: K = **44.60**, **82.5 speed points**. T has two modes on one build: the 15 runs of `ab`, the design before the cuts, span T 0.0681 to 0.0726.

**Current trial:** default workers capped at 10 instead of 12; SD_THREADS still overrides it. Quick 38/38, thread sweep 228/228, const-hash tiny 19/19, and official pre-push grid 43/43 pass. Board result pending.

**Check it yourself:** the board run named there, or locally `python3 tools/verify.py --tracks b`. Output SHA-256 on seed 1: `f86973c210228141bcb6f1f1b10188ad2a9b65f71e7cd7cf4590b38de4e80927`.

## Where it loses

- S is 1,077 code lines against the reference's 91, which costs (91/1,077)^0.15, K x 0.69, about 8 speed points. It's the most code of any of our tracks. Each 100 lines move K by about 1.4%.
- M scores nothing here. K takes min(M_ref / M, 50), and the board measured the reference at 2,199,961,600 bytes, so any M below 44.0 MB counts as 44.0. Ours has been below that since the design that stored postings (41.6 MB, `run-f39141d33527`), so cutting it from 42 to 27 MB with the block queries (D33) earned nothing by itself: K read 36.24 in that change's first run (`run-21faf3464957`), against 34.49 to 36.28 in the eight runs before it at S 1,015.
- Local timing ranks the block queries the wrong way round. With block queries at anchors holding N/96, N/48 and N/24 documents, local cycles read 2.10G, 2.13G and 2.16G, and the board read T 0.0996, 0.0909 and 0.0942, one sample each (D36). Our guess, not confirmed, is that a block's columns and tallies go through a cache that two threads share on the box. Locally the whole run takes about 40 ms (D37, D38; 41 to 42 ms for `ab` in the serial agent's runs), and on the box T is about 69 ms for `ab` (median 0.0690 over 15 runs).
- The board can't tell changes of under 1% apart: the 15 runs of `ab`, one build, span T 0.0681 to 0.0726 and K 42.05 to 43.43.
- The plan decides on one thread, with its phrases on a second, after a sample on 5 threads. Moving its decisions onto the scan's threads was tried and dropped (D38), and we haven't timed the plan on the box for this design. Locally, for `h12`, parse took 2.5 ms, the plan 4.5 and the scan 32 of about 41 (PROGRESS.md).
- Printing keeps an order: threads format batches of 1,024 queries, and the last thread to finish writes them all to the file in one call, so none waits for its turn (step 58). Printing at known offsets was tried and reverted (D26).

**What each phase cost on the box.** We can't run a profiler there, so a labelled `exp:` push ran one phase twice, on throwaway state, and T minus the usual T is that phase's wall time, against T of about 0.099:

| Run twice | Run | T (s) | Phase's wall time |
|---|---|---|---|
| The scan | `run-804bf7c7f1a1` | 0.1761 | about 77 ms |
| Parsing the queries and planning | `run-526859a8c142` | 0.1206 | about 22 ms, 7 to 13 locally |
| Answering and printing | `run-7baf964cdb45` | 0.1136 | about 15 ms, 6.7 locally |

These runs timed the build of D20, which still stored postings, and ran a round of threads for each phase. The rows sum to 114 ms, more than T, so they overstate: a phase run a second time finds the caches full of other data. The block queries have changed the scan since, and we haven't measured the phases of the current design this way.

## Where the reference spends its time

About 62% is building the index, with a set for every one of 609,833 terms, and 37% is the queries, where every operation builds a new set.

## Decision log

**D1. Start with threads.** Context: unlike A, C and D, the single-threaded version of this design has a different shape, since the postings need a merge. Options: one thread first, or threads straight away. Chose: threads, with equal byte ranges, because the merge then needs no sort. Cost: a slow thread slows the whole scan. Evidence: the sweep passes at 1, 2, 3, 7, 12 and 64 threads. Outcome: kept.

**D2. Anchor each phrase on its rarest term, not Aho-Corasick.** Context: phrases need matching against 36M tokens. Options: an automaton stepping on every token, or a check around each occurrence of one rare term. Chose: the anchor, which is simpler, and any term gives correct answers. A wrong guess from the sample only costs more checks. Cost: each document's tokens are buffered, which is small. Outcome: kept for now.

**D3. Chains of 2-byte offsets instead of a list and a final copy.** Context: v1 held each posting twice for a moment, 4 bytes in a thread's list and 4 in the final array. Options: compress the lists, or write postings straight to their final place. Chose: a chain of 32-byte blocks per term per thread, each with 14 offsets from a part's first document. Threads cover document order, so no merge or sort is needed. A part holds at most 65,535 documents, and running with few threads exercises several parts. Cost: reading a list now walks a chain, and S grew. Evidence: M 127.4 -> 99.3 MB, T 0.203 -> 0.194 s (local). Outcome: kept.

**D4. Reorder AND and NOT runs.** Context: answering took 55 of 195 ms, with every step reading its whole list, even with R already empty. AND and NOT both intersect, so a run of them between two ORs can go in any order, and the query's first term counts as an AND. Chose: the rarest ANDs first, by the sample's counts, then NOTs, skipping every step while R is empty. A wrong guess only costs time. Cost: 26 lines. Evidence: T 0.194 -> 0.175 s, M 99.3 -> 71.2 MB, K 17.73 -> 19.88 (local). Outcome: kept.

**D5. Move the bitmap threshold by measuring.** Context: we set the threshold from memory alone, at N/18. We then compared N/18, N/24, N/32, N/48 and N/64 on our scorer, and N/48 looked best. `ab.sh` over 9 runs gave -7.1% with 3.7% noise, just inside its twice-the-noise bar, so the board decided: T 0.297 -> 0.273 s, M 70.2 -> 72.9 MB, K 19.70 -> 20.40. Cost: one number. Outcome: kept.

**D6. Answer most BOOL queries in the scan.** Context: M was 73 MB, with the cap at 44 MB, and chains took most of it. Options: compress the chains, or not store them. Chose the second. Every document matching a BOOL query contains an anchor: the rarest AND of its last run, or else each OR term. So at an anchor's first occurrence in a document, each term's stamp (the last document holding it) gives the whole formula in six compares with no branch. ## The final design

- **Data layout.** A table of 64-byte sets of four terms: four short keys of 8 bytes, three long keys, a word for each term, and the word of no way, which a lookup that finds none gets, so no branch depends on it. The fourth way only takes terms of up to 8 bytes, which need no long key. One multiply hashes a key. Terms are numbered by how they're stored: those with rows first, then the others with columns, in order of first use so that a query's columns sit together, then the rest, the commonest first. A document's marks are a byte per term, equal to its stamp, its number in its block from 1 to 255, so nothing is cleared between documents. BOOL checks are truth tables over the marks of a query's other terms: up to three in a word of 8 bytes, up to five in 24. A term in a block query has a column, 256 bits over the block's documents. All heap comes from one region grown 64 MB at a time, so huge pages replace thousands of small faults.
- **How a query is answered.** A sample of 2 MiB, about 1% of the corpus, counts each term's occurrences. A BOOL query's anchors are terms of which every match holds at least one, chosen over the whole query: an OR adds its term, an AND replaces the anchors so far if its term holds no more documents than they do in all, and a NOT changes nothing. If the anchors hold about N/48 documents in all, by the sample, the query goes by blocks; otherwise each document that holds an anchor is checked at the anchor's first occurrence in it. A common term that alone makes a query match, an OR after the last AND or NOT, has its own count, which also goes by blocks if the query's other anchors are rare. The plan agent's simulation on seed 1, with the 2 MiB sample, puts 1,079 BOOL queries by blocks, with 2,117 columns; we haven't counted these in the build itself. A phrase is checked around each occurrence of its rarest term.
- **I/O.** Both files are mapped read-only, with zero padding so that block loads can run past the end. A helper thread, started first, creates the output file and faults in 12 MB of heap while the queries are parsed; once the sample is done it starts four threads that map the corpus's pages ahead with `MADV_POPULATE_READ`, and the tokenizer prefetches the line 16 blocks on. Output is formatted in batches of 1,024 queries, and the last thread to finish writes them all, in order, in one vectored call.
- **Parallelism.** The parse runs on every thread, the first of which makes the term table while the others read their lines; the sample runs on 5. One thread decides the plan, and one more takes its phrases. The scan's threads are started before the plan, from a thread of their own, and sleep on a read lock that the main thread holds until the plan is done; the main thread then scans too. Threads claim ranges of the corpus in order, each of a little under a block's documents (BLOCK_DOCS less 8, in bytes from the sample's mean document length), so that a range rarely cuts a block short, and a block ends where a range does. Each thread keeps a count and sum per tally, in one word: the count above bit 40, the sum of ids below. Summing them after the scan is deterministic. An answer's ten smallest ids sit behind a lock. A tally's place in the plan holds the last of the ten, read without the lock and only falling, so a document past it never takes the lock; a thread's first ten hits per tally can, and a phrase's match takes it every time, as matches are so few. The same threads scan, total and print.
- **The hot loop.** Per document: `token_edges` lists where tokens start and end, from 64-byte masks and a table per byte. `look_up` takes four tokens at a time: their keys, hashes and sets' addresses in AVX2, with no prefetch of the sets; then comparisons, eight words at a time, one vector compare per set, in a loop so short that the cache misses of many sets overlap; the words with no flags mark their terms, and the rest are listed by a table per mask, with no branch. A third pass gives the unsure words a second look. `mark` lists the first occurrence of each term with BOOL checks and the positions of phrase anchors, and sets a column's bit for terms that have columns without rows. A row is the document's marks of 64 terms with rows, as bits; every 64 documents `store_rows` transposes them into the columns' words. `check_phrases` compares up to 8 terms in one vector. `check_bools` runs the short checks in one loop, as a loop per anchor mispredicts its exit. When a block is full, `answer_block` runs each block query over the columns, 256 documents at a time, and counts and sums ids with nibble tables. Locally, `ab` took 2.672G instructions and 1.567G cycles on seed 1, about 1.7 instructions per cycle (D38's build: 2.869G); `s2-y` has not been counted on its own.

## How we know it's correct

- Every build is checked by SHA-256 against the naive reference on 15 generator seeds at tiny and sample, 4 answer-preserving rewrites of the input and 4 limit cases, at 1, 2, 3, 7, 12 and 64 threads, and on 5 seeds at full scale at the default thread count.
- A constant-hash test build, where every key collides, gives the same answers.
- Repeated runs at 2 to 14 threads, since the term table is filled by all threads at once, and races don't show on one pass: 300 runs with none wrong, before any change there goes out.
- An adversarial review of the build before D28 found no wrong answers: 960 race runs and hostile inputs against the reference. A second, of the final source, found no wrong answers in 226 fuzz cases, and one hang, now fixed (D68).
- A second review, of `077d9b5` with the block queries, stamps and mapper threads, tried to break it with the naive reference as the oracle, and found no wrong answer or crash on any input the spec allows: 11 fresh seeds at full scale, 86 at sample and 238 at tiny; 6,134 random small inputs in 53,746 runs at seven thread counts, seeing all 255 block sizes; every BOOL shape of 1 to 6 terms with repeats, over six corpora; 209 hand-built edges; forced plans. An input built to make threads parse the same new terms at once ran 4,500 times with none wrong, and caught planted races when it was run on them. To see what a pass is worth, 56 bugs were planted one at a time. The fuzz caught 49; of the other seven, two are races that the input above catches, one is caught by AddressSanitizer, three can't change the output, and one can only go wrong on a coincidence the tests didn't produce. The parse's unlocked look at the term table is formally a data race, which relies on x86's memory order; ThreadSanitizer reports it and nothing else that's ours. The review couldn't run on the box itself.
- What the reviews did break is outside the spec, and is listed in the [README](README.md#limits).
- **Reproduce it yourself:** `python3 tools/verify.py --full`, at the default thread count (Docker, git and Python only).

## With another 24 hours

- **Time the current phases on the box.** The phase table is from D20's build. A twice-run push for the scan, for the block queries alone and for parse and plan would show where the 78 ms go now, which local cycles can't (D36).
- **Fewer bytes through L2 per block.** D38 cut the columns read per block from 158 to 94 KB, and T went from 0.0810 (`run-dd406548283b`) to 0.0787 (`run-840d618b3bd5`) together with the other changes of that round. Smaller blocks, or fewer columns by sending fewer queries to blocks, are untried and could go either way: a pass over a query's columns costs the same however few documents the block has.
- **T has two modes on one build, and we haven't explained it.** `ab`'s 15 runs span T 0.0681 to 0.0726; curing the slow mode was worth about 2% of K (PROGRESS.md). One cause is found: late thread starts. Creating a thread's stack waits on the `mmap_lock` that the mappers' `madvise(MADV_POPULATE_READ)` holds, and readers of that lock can pass a waiting writer for up to 4 ms, so in `c-sr` four scan threads started 1 to 5 ms late on busy runs. `s2-y` starts the scan's threads first and lets other threads leave their stacks for the mappers (D67). Whether that closes the second mode takes more runs than its 5 to say.
- **Stacks made once, in one mapping.** Track C avoids the same wait with raw `pthread_create` on stacks it maps and touches itself. We haven't tried that on B, where the threads would then never take the lock.
- **Lines.** Each 100 lines are worth about 0.3 speed points. The nearest duplicates are the two shapes of check (`counts_packed` and `counts`) and the two ways of adding an id to an answer's smallest ten (`add_smallest` and `add_smallest_of_block`).
- **Memory is free up to 44.0 MB.** M is about 31.8 MB, so about 15 MB could buy time. The obvious uses lost: a larger term table (+4% worse, D37). A prefault of 12 MB by the helper stayed (step 60); one of 24 MiB put 585 us on the scan's start locally, and a 20 MB one read M 34 to 36 and T 0.072 on the board (PROGRESS.md).
